# 08 · The real site, v1

James asked to "see the real fucking site": live HTML text, real buttons, a real form, real scrolling, not
screenshots or crossfades of mock pages. This is that site. It is the reviewed mock beats (`mock/`, review round 2)
joined into one scrolling page, played from the rendered key frames that exist today. Nothing was re-rendered.

- **Entry:** `site/index.html`. Plain static files; no framework, no npm, no build step needed to serve it.
- **Serve:** `cd site && python3 -m http.server 8765` (any static host works; Vercel serves the folder as is).
- **Rebuild the HTML** after changing the beat table or the mock copy: `python3 tools/build_site.py`.
- **Check it:** serve it, then `node tools/shoot_site.cjs http://127.0.0.1:8765/` (screenshots + report, exit 1 on
  any problem) and `node ../susd-options/tools/copycheck.mjs site/index.html`.

## 1. What a visitor gets

One page, scroll only. Nothing has to be clicked to get the point; nothing runs away from the reader.

1. **First screen.** The ship's side with the welded sentence (the rendered frame), the audience line, the water tag
   "complexity: 28 pathways", and the Bootloader's own first question with its live readout: 8 people, 28
   coordination pathways, Team 8-12 and Michael's band line. The sentence is also the page's `<h1>` (screen readers).
2. **Scrolling hires.** The readout counts with the scroll (p = n(n-1)/2, with "+(n-1)" while one hire at a time is
   being added), the band and Michael's band line change at 13 and 15, and the frames crossfade from one key frame
   to the next: hiring (10), twelve, touch at 13 (red signal, the three questions), under (camera cut under water),
   Redline (39 people, 741 pathways), the turn, Side (cut: a new strake lifts the words), Down (water drops, green
   signal, capacity / margin / complexity tags), **the offer**, cycle 2, Repeat, Oxygen, and the finale.
3. **The Bootloader is on every screen.** Desktop: the dock top right. Phone: the bar at the bottom. Both step aside
   only while the mid-story offer or the finale form is on screen, so each screen has one button.
4. **"Get it free"** (dock, bar, skip link, offer button) jumps straight to the finale `#get` (an instant jump, not a
   smooth scroll through the whole story). With a mouse or keyboard the email field takes focus; on touch it does
   not (the keyboard would cover what the file is before it is read).
5. **The finale form.** An empty or malformed address shows "Enter your email address." under the field. A real
   address shows "Taking you to the Bootloader page...", fades in the drained-water frame, and after 0.8 s
   (0.3 s with reduced motion) opens `https://scaling-up-side-down.vercel.app/appendix#get` **in the same tab**.
   Nothing is sent anywhere: the field has no `name`, the form's submit is handled in script, the address never
   leaves the page (the shooter checks the navigation URL carries no email).
   *Why the same tab:* the finale is the end of the story, so there is nothing to come back to mid-way, and Back
   still returns here; a new tab opened after the drain's delay is not a direct result of the tap, so iOS Safari and
   the in-app browsers James uses would block it as a pop-up; and one tab keeps the "one button, one next step" rule.
6. **The book** is the second path: "The book" link in the title lockup goes to the book line at the foot of the
   finale ("The book by Michael Wentz. Pre-orders open soon."). "Read the Appendix" links to the absolute
   Appendix URL (the preview runs on another host).

Every sentence is Michael's, verbatim, with its source ref in an HTML comment beside it (copied from the mock's
`PAGES` table and block functions in `mock/build_pages.py`). Labels and buttons are marked `// label`.

## 3. How playback works

**One clock.** `s` = scroll position through the story in vh, measured against the runway's real height
(`(scrollY - storyTop) / (runwayHeight / 1260)`), so it stays right on iOS where `vh` and the toolbar disagree.
The stage is `position: sticky; top: 0; height: 100svh`; the runway after it is `1260svh` long; the finale section
scrolls in over the last frame after it. Native scrolling only: no scroll hijacking, no smoothing library.

**One beat table.** `SECTIONS` in `tools/build_site.py` is the only place the story is timed:

| beat | frame (desktop / phone) | frame fully on at s | caption on (s) | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hero | d01_hero_K1 / p01 | 0 | 0-45 | first paint, eager |
| hire | d15_hire_n10 / p01 (held) | 80 | 55-128 | n = 10 |
| twelve | d02_twelve_K2 / p03 | 160 | 138-205 | n = 12 |
| touch | d03_touch_K3 / p04 | 240 | 215-312 | n = 13, the three questions |
| under | d04_under_K5 / p02 | 330, **cut** | 325-425 | n 14 to 19 |
| redline | d05_redline_K6 / p05 | 450 | 440-540 | n = 39; readout fades out at 545 |
| turn | d06_turn_K7 / p06 | 560 | 550-632 | |
| side | d07_side1_K8 / p07 (held) | 650, **cut** | 642-725 | cycle legend on |
| down | d08_down1_K9 / p07 | 750 | 735-820 | |
| offer | holds d08 / p07 | (no frame) | 830-905 | dock and bar step aside |
| up2 | d09_up2_K10 / p07 (held) | 920 | (none) | frame only |
| cycle2 | d10_side2_K11 / p07 (held) | 990 | 915-1025 | "Cycle 2 of 3" |
| repeat | d11_up3_K13 / p07 (held) | 1060 | 1035-1120 | "Cycle 3 of 3" |
| oxygen | d12_oxygen_K16 / p08 | 1150 | 1135-1225 | |
| fin | d13_finale_K16F / p09 | 1230 | (none) | the ground under the finale |

`build_site.py` writes each row onto its section as `data-fs` (frame fully on), `data-cut`, `data-cin` / `data-cout`
(caption window) and `data-a` (the anchors the player needs, desktop and phone), and writes the rest of the timing
(the readout's n along the story, the readout / legend / three-questions windows, the lit cycle phase) into
`#story-config`. `stage.js` reads only those; it holds no timing of its own.

**Each scroll frame** (`requestAnimationFrame`, only when the scroll changes), `render()`:

1. `frameState(s)` finds the two neighbouring key frames A, B and the blend t: each frame holds, then the next one
   blends in over `F = 30` vh before its `fs`. Across a cut, or with reduced motion, t snaps to 0 or 1. A frame that
   has not loaded yet is never shown half-blended: the nearest loaded frame holds instead.
2. `drawFrames(A, B, t)` sets `--fo` (opacity) on each section's frame. **This is the only function that paints
   frames.**
3. Captions: opacity from the caption window, fading over `fc = 10` vh at each end (`--lo`), `visibility: hidden`
   when off so nothing invisible can be clicked or read twice.
4. The readout: n interpolated from the config's `[s, n]` points and rounded, p = n(n-1)/2, the band switched at 13
   and 15; its position follows the waterline of the frame(s) on screen (interpolated across the blend). The screen
   reader copy (`aria-live="polite"`) changes only when the band changes, at most every 1.5 s.
5. The water tag rides the waterline between frames and carries "complexity: N pathways" while the readout is on.
   It hides rather than sit on a caption.
6. The dock / bar hide while the offer (caption opacity above 0.3) or the form (30 % in view, IntersectionObserver)
   is on screen.

**Positions.** The frame box (`.fbox`) is the rendered frame scaled to cover the stage, top-aligned, centred on the
sentence when the viewport is narrower than 16:10 (`--bw`, `--bh`, `--bx`, `--by` in `site.css`, in container query
units). Every anchored thing (tags, captions, the readout) is placed in % of that box from `out/stills/*.anchors.json`,
so the HTML stays on the rendered waterline and clear of the rendered sentence at any size. `fitLayers()` (on load
and resize) moves a caption up if it would run under the phone bar or off the bottom, never onto the sentence or the
rendered load-line mark, and scales it down (not below 0.72) only if moving is not enough.

**Loading.** The first frame (`fetchpriority="high"`), the CSS and two preloaded fonts are all the first paint
needs. After `load`, the other frames load in story order, two at a time (each section gets class `ld`, which
reveals its `<img>`; until then the lazy image never requests). The drain frame loads when the form comes near.
Measured bytes before first paint: see section 6.

**Reduced motion** (`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, or `?reduced=1`): no crossfades; frames and captions switch at
the middle of each blend. Scrolling is still the only control.

**No JavaScript:** the `html.js` class is never set, so every beat renders as a plate in normal flow, one after
another: its own frame with its own words, tags, readout (or cycle legend) and caption, top to bottom, then the
finale. On the phone each plate is the whole portrait frame tall and the readout is one line above the bar (as on the
reviewed phone beat pages). The form still submits (GET to the Appendix URL; without script the browser does it).

**Phone** (any viewport taller than 3:4): the portrait frames (p01-p09) where they exist; where a beat has none it
holds the nearest portrait frame that reads right (see the table) and hides the water tags, because that frame's
water is not this beat's water. Title strip on top, Bootloader bar at the bottom, the readout docks into one line
above the bar once scrolling starts, captions are 350 px wide and stop 14 px short of the rendered draught marks.

## 4. Dropping in rendered sequences later (brief 9.1-9.3)

The page is built so a rendered frame sequence replaces the crossfades without touching the clock, the captions,
the readout or the chrome:

1. Pack the frames as the brief says (7.8: WebP lo/hi chunks + manifest under `site/assets/seq/{d,p}/{lo,hi}/`), with
   each frame's `s` (and `cam`) in the manifest.
2. Add one `<canvas aria-hidden="true">` to `.stage` above the `.beat` frames (z-index 1).
3. Replace `drawFrames(fst)` in `stage.js` with a canvas painter: find the two sequence frames around `s`, draw A,
   then B at `globalAlpha = t` (no blend across a `cam` change), cover transform on the same frame box (`G.bx`,
   `G.by`, `G.bw`, `G.bh` are already measured), nearest decoded frame if one is missing. Keep the stills as the
   poster / fallback: leave the hero still in place for first paint and hide the stills once the canvas has drawn.
4. Replace `want()` / `pump()` with the brief's FrameStore (lo for all frames first, then hi outward from the
   playhead, two chunks at a time, `createImageBitmap`).
5. Leave `frameState()`'s contract (`{A, B, t}`) and the beat table alone: captions and the readout are timed on `s`,
   not on frames. If the sequence's waterline moves differently from the key frames, add the per-frame waterline to
   the manifest and feed it to `topOf()` / the water tag instead of the per-section anchors.

## 5. Decisions taken here (where the brief or the mocks left a choice)

- **Same-tab handoff** for the form (section 1.5).
- **Native scroll**, no Lenis: no dependencies, nothing from other hosts, and nothing that fights the user.
- **Instant jump** for "Get it free" (a smooth scroll through 1260 vh of story would replay the whole story).
- **No `<video>`, nothing from other hosts**: the drain is a still that fades in; images, fonts, CSS and JS are
  same-origin static files (the shooter fails on any request to another host except the Appendix handoff).
- The offer is placed after the first full cycle (Down), over the held Down frame; cycle 2 and 3 follow.

## 7. Known gaps

- **Crossfades, not motion.** Between key frames the picture cross-dissolves; the water does not rise frame by
  frame. The numbers count smoothly, the picture steps. Section 4 is the path to the rendered sequence.
- **Phone frames are sparse** (9 portrait renders). Hiring holds the n = 8 portrait frame (the readout says 10);
  Side, Down, the offer, cycle 2 and Repeat all hold the Down portrait frame (p07), so on the phone the water does
  not show cycle 2 and 3 changing; the cycle legend is desktop only (as in the mocks).
- **Images are JPEG stills** (120-260 KB each, 3.7 MB in all), not the brief's lo/hi WebP tiers; after first paint
  they load two at a time in story order. On a slow phone, a fast scroller can reach a beat before its frame: the
  nearest loaded frame holds until it arrives (never a blank or a half blend).
- **The email goes nowhere** (the MVP decision): the Appendix page is where the file is sent. The note under the
  field says so ("Opens the Bootloader page, where the file is sent by email.").
- **Checked in Chromium only**; iOS Safari itself has not been run here (the layout uses `svh`, container query
  units, `:is()` and `text-wrap`, all in Safari 16.4+/17). Worth one look on James's iPhone before it goes wide.
- **Rendered-mark check is approximate**: the shooter tests drawn text lines against boxes around the load-line
  mark and the draught marks, sized from measurements of two stills; a mark under the water seen from above is not
  counted. It catches real collisions (section 8) but a hand look at the shots is still the test.
- **Phone finale, after the landing position:** the finale scrolls over the fixed last frame, so once the reader
  scrolls past `#get` its lines can pass over the rendered marks and the stencil. Only the landing position is
  checked. One case of this is built in: on a 390 x 664 phone an empty submit scrolls about 45 px so the error line
  shows under the button, and for that moment the lead line passes over the hull's "SUSD BOOTLOADER" stencil.
- **Line breaks:** the desktop offer's spec line "35,445 words for your AI to read, so you don't have to" breaks
  before "have to" (as in the reviewed mock); at 1280 and below the finale's "...provided with it." leaves "with it."
  on its own line.
- **Viewports narrower than 16:10** (iPad landscape, 1280 x 1024): the frame is cropped on the right, so the gauge
  and its tick labels go off screen; the player hides a label rather than let the screen edge cut it. Below 1200 px
  the dock drops its "Works with Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini" line so it stays off the words.
