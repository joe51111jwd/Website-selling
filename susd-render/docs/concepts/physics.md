# Physical truth: two homepage concepts for *Scaling Up-Side-Down*

**Lens.** Find a real physical system whose behaviour *is* the book's argument. Render that system
photographically in Blender (real materials, real sun, a real lens), and put Michael's words
physically on the object that performs the behaviour. The visitor should understand the argument
the way they understand a cup falling off a table: by seeing it happen, not by reading a diagram.

**The two concepts**

| id | Name | The image in one line |
|---|---|---|
| physics-1 | **The Fold** | The title, printed on one sheet of card, folded at its two hyphens, stands on its edge and carries a hardcover on the two creases. |
| physics-2 | **The Lifts** | A wall of rammed earth built in twelve layers, with "Up. Side. Down. Repeat." [M 1713 · copy.ts:125] cut into the layers; beside it, the same earth poured in one go, which slumps into a heap when the forms come off. |

**References.** `[BL n]` is line *n* of `susd-options/source/public_bootloader.md`. `[M n · copy.ts:k]`
is manuscript line *n* as recorded in `susd-options/source/src_copy.ts` line *k*. Every quote in
straight double quotes below was checked against its line by `susd-render/tools/check_meaning_quotes.py`
(result in section 5). Words in curly quotes (“ ”) are James, labels or my own notes, never site copy.

## 0. What “AI made” meant, and what this lens does about it

Last night's four options (the-title, count-yourself, waterline, corrected-edition) are flat CSS on a
cream or green ground: paper grain overlays, Fraunces/Newsreader italics, Archivo/Bricolage, small
tracked mono caps labels, pill buttons, a centred eyebrow/headline/sub/CTA stack, and depth faked with
drop shadows or a CSS water fill. James reads that whole vocabulary as “AI made”. The one real render we
had (the wooden blocks) had depth but no message: “you just look at the blocks and don't read the text”.

This lens answers both complaints with one move: **the depth and the message are the same object**.
The words are printed on the card that stands up, or cut into the wall that stands up. You cannot look
at the picture without reading the words, because the words are what is standing. The page background is
the photograph itself, never a CSS colour or a grain texture.

Rules both concepts keep:
- No cream paper ground, no grain overlay, no pills, no tracked mono labels, no eyebrow/sub stack.
- Every sentence on screen is Michael's, word for word, with its line. Labels and buttons are the only fresh text.
- The physics is real and checkable at home (a sheet of card and a book; a bucket of damp soil and a box).
- Nothing in either picture is a cliché the brief bans (no rockets, stairs, arrows, gears, dominoes,
  Jenga, puzzle pieces, origami cranes, mountain climbers).

## 2. physics-1: The Fold

### The image
The book's title, printed in big black letterpress type on one sheet of heavy white card, folded at its
two hyphens into three standing panels (UP, SIDE, DOWN), carrying a heavy hardcover that rests on the two
creases.

### Why it is the book
1. **Michael defines the title as three dimensions.** Up is the Y axis, "Growth (the axis everyone obsesses over)" [BL 808];
   Side is the X axis, "Stabilization (the axis no one sees)" [BL 809]; Down is the Z axis, "Optimization (the axis everyone fears)" [BL 810].
   A flat sheet has height and width. Folding gives it the third, depth. Down is literally the
   dimension that makes it stand: the axis everyone fears is the one that holds the weight.
2. **The flat sheet is his picture of the problem.** "One-dimensional scaling looks like progress but it's just a line getting longer." [BL 812]
3. **The folded sheet is his picture of the fix.** "Three-dimensional scaling builds a structure. Each cycle adds depth, width, and height. The business has volume. It has weight. It can hold load." [BL 814]
   and "You can't build a building with only height. You need all three dimensions or it topples." [BL 816]
4. **The hyphens are the folds, and the folds carry the load.** "The hyphens aren't decoration—they're the rhythm." [BL 795]
   Flatten one fold and it falls: "Skip a leg, tip upside down." [BL 795]
5. **The spelling is the physics.** Michael's vocabulary lock makes the unhyphenated "Scaling Upside Down" [BL 3960] the
   problem and "Scaling Up-Side-Down" [BL 3961] the framework. A sheet printed without hyphens has nowhere to fold.
   It cannot stand.
6. **It answers James directly.** The page that was flat physically stops being flat. That is the first thing the site does.

### The physics (true, and checkable with a sheet of card)
- Stiffness against bending grows with the square of depth (second moment of area). The same 0.4 mm card,
  flat and folded 13 cm deep, differs in bending stiffness by tens of thousands of times (order of magnitude).
- In a folded plate the fold lines work as columns. A panel edge with no fold has nothing holding it in line
  and buckles first (a plate with one free edge buckles at roughly a tenth of the load of one held on both
  edges). So flattening one crease turns two panels into one wide unsupported leaf: it bows, the book slides,
  the sheet tips.
- A paper column holding books is a school demonstration nearly everyone has seen, so a stranger believes it
  instantly.
- **Reference shoot (do this first):** cut a 600 × 210 mm sheet of 300 gsm card, score it at 200 mm and 400 mm,
  fold it into a zig-zag, stand it, lay a 1 kg hardcover across the two creases, then flatten one crease. Film it
  on a phone at 60 fps from the hero angle. The render must match what that camera sees (how the creases round
  off, how the panels bow, how the book slides). This is our realism check, not site content.

It says a business that only grows up topples, and one that grows up, sideways and down stands and carries
weight, like this page folded at its hyphens. I can pre-order the book or get a free file.”

### The dramatic action
**The page stands up.** On load, the flat title sheet folds itself at its two hyphens, rises onto its edge
and takes the weight of the book. It is the whole argument in 2.5 seconds: flat cannot hold anything;
three dimensions can. The opt-in echoes it: submitting the email folds the Bootloader sheet into walls.

### Type
- **Display, made by us: “Crease”.** Every letter is drawn as one strip of card folded flat: constant
  stroke width, no curves, every change of direction a mitred 45° fold with a hairline seam at the joint
  (so C, G, O, S, D, P are octagonal folds). Heavy and wide, built for letterpress at 100–200 mm. The hyphen
  is wider than normal and carries a hairline crease mark down its centre, so on the card the paper's real
  crease lands in the glyph's own crease. Built with a Python generator (stroke skeleton → mitred outline →
  UFO → fontmake → OTF/WOFF2). The same outlines go to Blender as the ink mask and deboss height map, so the
  type on the card and the type on the page are one drawing.
- **Text: Format 1452** (Velvetyne, OFL), a DIN-like face “built with modules, without any optical
  corrections”: engineered, rarely seen on the web, nothing like Fraunces/Archivo/Inter. Sentence case,
  normal tracking. Check its em dash, curly apostrophe and figures before committing.

### Blender plan
- **Geometry.** Card: a 600 × 210 mm plane, 300 × 105 quads, solidify 0.4 mm, three panels by vertex group.
  Two hinge empties at the crease lines drive the fold; a 6 mm band either side of each crease bends through
  a simple-deform so creases are rounded, not hinged. Panel bow and the skip-a-leg buckle as shape keys.
  Book: rounded-spine hardcover with page block. Table: 1.4 × 0.8 m slab. Wall: plane 1.2 m behind. Window
  gobo: a plane with four pane cut-outs between the sun and the set.
- **Materials (CC0).** Card: ambientCG Paper002 or Paper005 (bump and roughness only; colour set to #F2F2EE),
  diffuse mixed with 12% translucent; ink from 4K masks rendered from the Crease outlines, roughness 0.35,
  deboss displacement 0.15 mm, crack mask along creases. Slate: Poly Haven `slate_floor_02`, roughness raised
  to a honed 0.55. Wall: Poly Haven `grey_plaster` or `rough_plaster_03`. Book cloth: procedural linen weave
  plus foil (metallic mask) for the spine. HDRI: Poly Haven `artist_workshop` or `small_workshop` at 0.3.
- **Light.** Sun angle 0.6°, 5000 K, 25° elevation from front-left through the gobo; HDRI for fill only.
- **Simulation.** Intro fold and book settle: keyframed from the reference video (deterministic). Flat sheet
  falling and skip-a-leg: Bullet rigid bodies (panels, book) with hinge constraints at the creases, then baked

- **Frames and time** (Cycles CPU, OIDN; 24 samples ≈ 30–35 s per 1440 × 900 frame on our 4 cores):

## 3. physics-2: The Lifts

### The image
A rammed-earth wall built in twelve coloured layers, with "Up. Side. Down. Repeat." [M 1713 · copy.ts:125] cut deep into the
layers in raking sun; beside it, the same earth poured into the same forms in one go, which slumps into a heap
the moment the forms come off.

### Why it is the book
1. **Rammed earth is built in exactly three motions per layer, then repeated.** Pour loose soil into the form
   (the level rises: Up), rake it level from side to side (Side), ram it down until it is dense (Down). Then the
   next layer, on top. Michael: "Up. Side. Down. Repeat." [M 1713 · copy.ts:125]
2. **Each layer must be finished before the next.** A rammer only compacts the top 15–20 cm, so builders lay soil
   in thin layers. Michael: "Cyclical Growth assumes gains come from completed rotations — Up stretches, Side absorbs, Down locks." [BL 870]
   and "Nothing moves forward until the cycle completes. But when it does, the next cycle starts higher." [BL 870]
3. **New material is load before it is strength.** Loose soil just pushes on the forms; only rammed does it
   carry anything. That is his mechanism: "Every hire compounds complexity before it compounds capacity." [M 696 · copy.ts:60]
   and "Each hire is a microdose of complexity. The cycle processes it before the next dose." [BL 1364]
4. **One layer per person, twelve layers.** The Bootloader counts "12 USD mini-cycles" as "Each person = one complete cycle to integrate" [BL 2217]
   and says Rented Scale reaches Team in "8-12 mini S-curves" [BL 1362] because "Integration happens sequentially." [BL 1362]
   A twelve-layer wall is that path, built one integration at a time. (Bought Scale is "1 large S-curve" [BL 1361]:
   the one big pour, which only survives with forms heavy enough to hold it, because "Capital absorbs integration cost." [BL 1361])
5. **The failure is accumulation, not height.** Both walls are the same height, same earth, same tools. The
   rushed one fails because it was filled faster than it was compacted, which is Michael's exact diagnosis:
   "Complexity that accumulated faster than the structure could hold." [M 2021 · copy.ts:96]
   This avoids the misreading that a tall thing falls just for being tall.
6. **The rushed wall is his story told by soil.** "Always launching. Always hiring. Always adding." [M 1993 · copy.ts:72]
   "But inside, nothing ever settled." [M 1995 · copy.ts:73] Inside the forms, nothing was ever compacted.
7. **Same earth, so the founder is not the problem.** The material and the effort were identical; only the
   method differed. "You were never the problem. The model you inherited was." [M 216 · copy.ts:196] The Bootloader says it of
   the two kinds of scale: "Same game. Different rules." [BL 243]
8. **The layers are the deposits.** The finished wall is a visible record of completed cycles, each one on the
   last: "Each cycle deposits systems and margin. The next cycle withdraws from a larger account." [BL 866]
   And the heap is literally a pile, Michael's own word for gains that don't compound (Library vs. Pile, BL 2622-2626):
   "Gains that aren't locked in structure leak away." [BL 868]
9. **The Bootloader is the formwork.** Forms don't build the wall; they contain it while you ram it. "This is not a playbook. It is a constraint layer." [BL 213]
   "Do not treat this document as steps to follow—treat it as walls that contain." [BL 215]

### The physics (true; checkable with a bucket of damp soil and a box)
- Rammed earth is placed as loose, slightly damp soil in layers (“lifts”) of roughly 150–200 mm and rammed to
  roughly half to two-thirds of that depth before the next lift goes in. Compaction does not reach deep loose
  fill, which is why lifts are thin.
- Properly rammed earth stands as soon as it is rammed: the forms can be stripped straight away.
- Uncompacted soil, unsupported, slumps toward its angle of repose (about 30–40°): it gets wider, not taller.
- Finished walls show each lift as a band, because each batch of soil differs slightly in colour and density,
  with a denser, darker skin at the top of each lift where the rammer struck.
- (General construction knowledge; none of these figures goes on the site. If any becomes a label, check it
  against a rammed-earth construction guide first.)

### The dramatic action
**Strip the forms.** The same earth, poured two ways; the boards come off both at once; the wall built in
layers stands and the wall poured in one go collapses into a heap. It plays once at real speed when the beat
pins (about 3 s) and reverses if you scroll back, rather than being scrubbed frame by frame, so it stays
smooth. The opt-in echoes it with a single rammer strike.

### Type
- **Display, made by us: “Lift”.** A wide, heavy, monumental sans drawn on a twelve-course grid (cap height =
  twelve units, one per lift). Stems and bars are smooth; every curve and diagonal is built as stacked

### Blender plan
- **Geometry.** Twelve lift slabs (6.0 × 0.45 m, heights 140–160 mm). The visible face uses adaptive
  subdivision (dicing rate 1 px) for true displacement in stills; sequences use a pre-baked mid-resolution
  displaced mesh. Inscription: the Lift outlines as curves, extruded 40 mm, exact boolean difference into the
  finished wall; edge crumble by noise displacement plus about 200 small crumbs settled by a short rigid-body
  run. Forms: film-faced plywood panels, steel walers, tie rods with wing nuts, clamps. A hand tamper (steel
  plate, ash handle) and a wheelbarrow. The rushed wall: about 3,000 rigid-body clods (convex hulls, 10–80 mm)
  packed into the form by a 60-frame settle, plus a top skin mesh.
- **Materials (CC0).** Soil mixes from Poly Haven `excavated_soil_wall`, `dense_sand`, `dry_ground_01`,
  `red_laterite_soil_stones` (and ambientCG Ground sets), blended per lift by a world-Z band function with
  hue and value shifts; plywood from Poly Haven `plywood` under a brown semi-gloss phenolic coat; steel from
  `metal_plate`. HDRI: Poly Haven `hilltop_construction` or `sunset_in_the_chalk_quarry`.
- **Light.** Sun angle 0.5°, 4500 K, 20° high, grazing 78° from the wall normal; HDRI for sky and fill.
- **Simulation.** Strip: form panels keyframed away; Bullet sim on the clods (friction 0.9, high damping for
  damp cohesive soil) with a slump shape key on the core mass toward 35°, baked once (≈ 15 min).
  Deterministic, so it repeats exactly. One lift: keyframed fill level, rake, tamper strikes, compaction as a
  scale on the lift's height with displacement change. Wall growth: one frame per lift count.
- **Frames and time** (heavier displacement, so ≈ 40–45 s per 1440 × 900 frame at 24 spp):
