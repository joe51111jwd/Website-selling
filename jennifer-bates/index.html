The category

What is Stakeholder Risk?

Stakeholder Risk is the enterprise risk that comes from the relationships, incentives, governance and decision rights among the people who decide a company’s outcome: founders, executives, boards, investors and lenders.

It sits upstream of everything a model measures. When those people pull apart, execution stalls, valuation comes under pressure and enterprise value erodes, often by seven to nine figures, while every line of the spreadsheet still checks out.

It is

  • Enterprise risk, measured and benchmarked
  • Diligence on the system, not the individuals
  • Priced into raises, deals and exits

It isn’t

  • Organizational consulting
  • Executive coaching
  • HR advisory

FAQ

Questions, answered

Who is Jennifer G. Bates?

Jennifer G. Bates is the founder and CEO of HumanFactor™ and the creator of Stakeholder Risk, a category of enterprise risk that comes from the people who decide a company’s outcome. A six-time founder based in New York, she has advised CEOs, boards, investors and fiduciaries for more than 20 years across life sciences, technology, finance and private equity.

What is Stakeholder Risk?

Stakeholder Risk is the enterprise risk that comes from the relationships, incentives, governance and decision rights among the people who decide a company’s outcome: founders, executives, boards, investors and lenders. It sits upstream of execution and valuation: when those people are misaligned, the plan stalls and the multiple compresses, even if the model never changes.

How is it different from management diligence?

Management diligence assesses individuals; Stakeholder Risk measures the system between them. A team of strong executives can still carry high Stakeholder Risk if decision rights overlap, incentives pull apart or the board and CEO want different outcomes.

Isn’t this HR, coaching or culture work?

No. Stakeholder Risk is enterprise risk, measured and priced like any other risk to value, and the work is done for CEOs, boards and investors, not for HR. It is not organizational consulting, executive coaching or HR advisory.

How is Stakeholder Risk measured?

With the HumanFactor Index™, which scores a company from 0 to 100 on twelve risks and four diagnostics (leadership identity, authority structure, decision architecture and stakeholder alignment), benchmarked against a database of more than 350,000 leaders. A higher score means more observed exposure.

When should a company look at it?

Before the moments when the people around the table change: a raise, an acquisition, a CEO transition, a new board, or an exit. Investors also use it before close and to monitor a portfolio.
