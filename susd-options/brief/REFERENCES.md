## The five-second test every reference was held to

A stranger lands on the page. Within five seconds, without scrolling, can they say:
1. **What it claims** (one sentence they could repeat),
2. **Whether it is about them** (they recognise themselves, or know they are not the audience),
3. **What to do next** (one obvious action)?

The references below pass because the *idea* is the thing you look at: a sentence set large, a
number, a question you answer, or a picture that *is* the claim (not a picture next to it).

## A. Explorable explanations: you learn the idea by touching it

### A1. Parable of the Polygons (Vi Hart and Nicky Case, 2014)
- **URL:** https://ncase.me/polygons/  · `Checked`
- **First 5 seconds:** The title, then one sentence: "This is a story of how harmless choices can make
  a harmful world." Under it, a small grid of cute triangles and squares, some unhappy. You are told
  to drag the unhappy ones. Within seconds you have *caused* the outcome the essay is about.
- **Technique:** A "playable post". The rule is stated in one sentence; the reader enacts it with one
  gesture; a tiny local rule produces a large, surprising global result. Later sliders let you run the
  same rule at scale automatically.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** The book's claim has exactly this shape: each hire is harmless, the sum is
  not. Let the visitor add people one at a time and watch the coordination web thicken. The line to
  put above it is already written: "Each hire is a microdose of complexity. The cycle processes it
  before the next dose." (BL L1364).

### A2. The Evolution of Trust (Nicky Case, 2017)
- **URL:** https://ncase.me/trust/  · `Known` (title confirmed; content from memory)
- **First 5 seconds:** A hand-drawn title card, a short story opening (the Christmas truce of 1914),
  then a single choice: cooperate or cheat. You play one round before you are told any theory.
- **Technique:** Game as argument. You feel the rule in your own decision, then the essay zooms out to
  simulations of many players. The conclusion is earned, not asserted.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** One decision, not a game: at 12 people, do you hire again, systemise, or
  cut? Each choice plays out in the picture (Up adds a node and 12+ new lines; Side bundles lines into
  systems; Down removes load). Caution: James said "it can't look like a game" [#13]. Keep the
  mechanic, drop any game chrome (scores, avatars, levels).

### A3. Ten Brighter Ideas and "Explorable Explanations" (Bret Victor, 2011)
- **URL:** https://worrydream.com/TenBrighterIdeas/ and https://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/  · `Checked` (by search)
- **First 5 seconds:** A plain list of energy claims. The numbers inside the sentences are live: drag
  one and every consequence in the paragraph recomputes. Victor calls it a "reactive document": the
  reader tests the author's assumptions and sees the consequences.
- **Technique:** Variables embedded in prose (his Tangle library). The text stays the hero; the
  interaction is a number inside it.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** The cleanest way to keep the words as the focus *and* make it interactive.
  The visitor sets one number (team size) and the page's numbers react (pathways, zone). Copy rule:
  the sentence stays verbatim; only the numerals beside it are live UI.

### A4. Mechanical Watch (Bartosz Ciechanowski, 2022)
- **URL:** https://ciechanow.ski/mechanical-watch/  · `Known`
- **First 5 seconds:** Calm prose in a single reading column, and directly under the first paragraph a
  clean WebGL watch you can rotate, with one slider. No hero banner, no chrome.
- **Technique:** Every figure demonstrates *exactly* the sentence above it and changes one variable at a
  time. Neutral palette, precise light, no decoration. Award-level craft that never competes with the
  text because each picture is the proof of the line you just read.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** The discipline James is asking for: "the visual serves the sentence".
  Each figure on the homepage answers the sentence above it (the web proves "66 pathways"; the curve
  proves "Complexity > Capacity = Redline."), with team size the only variable.

### A5. LOOPY (Nicky Case, 2017)
- **URL:** https://ncase.me/loopy/  · `Known`
- **First 5 seconds:** A tiny system of circles and arrows already running: pulses travel round a loop
  and the circles grow and shrink. You can draw your own.
- **Technique:** A cycle shown as *motion through a loop*, not as a static arrow diagram. Pulses make
  "this feeds that" legible without words.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** Up, Side, Down as three nodes with a pulse travelling round them and the
  business (a dot or a number) growing a step each lap. "Growth comes FROM completing cycles
  (Up/Side/Down), not from pushing through them." (BL L241).

## B. Newsroom explainers: one claim, one picture, proven in front of you

### B1. "Why outbreaks like coronavirus spread exponentially" (Harry Stevens, Washington Post, 14 Mar 2020)
- **URL:** https://www.washingtonpost.com/graphics/2020/world/corona-simulator/  · `Checked` (by search)
- **First 5 seconds:** The headline states the claim, then a box of 200 bouncing dots (one sick) with a
  small area chart drawing itself above the box as the dots infect each other. Reported as the most-read
  story in the Post's online history.
- **Technique:** A tiny agent simulation and a live chart *generated by the simulation*. The same
  simulation is re-run four times with one parameter changed. Stevens said it was meant to show how
  networks and exponential growth work, not to forecast.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** "Complexity grows faster than capacity" is a networks-and-growth claim. A
  field of people where each new person connects to everyone already there, with a two-line chart
  (people vs pathways) drawing itself above. Re-run at 8, 12, 15 and 40.

### B2. "What's Really Warming the World?" (Eric Roston and Blacki Migliozzi, Bloomberg, Jun 2015)
- **URL:** https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2015-whats-warming-the-world/  · `Checked` (by search)
- **First 5 seconds:** The headline is a question. One line chart of temperature since 1880. As you
  scroll, suspects (orbit, sun, volcanoes, ozone, deforestation) are laid over it one at a time and
  ruled out; greenhouse gases match. Bloomberg's most-read piece of 2015 (over 2 million uniques).
- **Technique:** A lineup and process of elimination on a *single* chart. The headline question is
  answered by the shape on screen.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** Founders blame themselves. Rule out the suspects on one chart and land on
  the one that fits: "Burnout is structural (Today/Tomorrow/Toys unseparated), not personal weakness"
  and "Coordination cost is physics (Graičiūnas), not a leadership failure" (BL L264, L265), closing on
  "You were never the problem. The model you inherited was." (M L216).

### B3. "You Draw It: How Family Income Predicts Children's College Chances" (Aisch, Cox, Quealy, NYT Upshot, 28 May 2015)
- **URL:** https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2015/05/28/upshot/you-draw-it-how-family-income-affects-childrens-college-chances.html  · `Checked` (by search)
- **First 5 seconds:** A question and a blank chart. You are asked to draw the line yourself. Only then
  is the real line revealed, next to your guess and other readers' guesses. Many readers guessed wrong,
  which is the point.
- **Technique:** Commit, then reveal. The gap between your guess and the truth is the lesson, and you
  own it because you drew it.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** Ask the visitor to draw how coordination grows from 1 to 40 people. Almost
  everyone draws a straight line. Reveal n(n-1)/2 and the capacity line under it; the crossing is the
  redline: "Complexity > Capacity = Redline." (M L2997). The strongest single "aha" available for this
  book.

### B4. "What Is Code?" (Paul Ford, Bloomberg Businessweek, 11 Jun 2015)
- **URL:** https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2015-paul-ford-what-is-code/  · `Checked` (by search)
- **First 5 seconds:** Giant type and a playful, living page around a 38,000-word essay. The page knows
  how fast you are reading, drops interactive examples in at the right moment, and gives a certificate
  of completion at the end.
- **Technique:** A book-length text treated as an *object with a personality*: it counts, reacts and
  rewards. Proof that very long writing can have a homepage that is fun to look at.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** The Bootloader is 35,445 words. Show it as a real file (version, word count,
  sections) that reacts, not as a prompt or a box: "The pages are reference. The file is the operating
  system." (M L4497). The opt-in becomes the "certificate" moment ("Yours").

### B5. "The Uber Game" (Robin Kwong and FT team, Financial Times, Oct 2017)
- **URL:** https://ig.ft.com/uber-game/  · `Checked` (by search)
- **First 5 seconds:** Second person: you are a full-time Uber driver. Pick a character, then face
  choices with earnings and hours meters ticking.
- **Technique:** Role-play choices with consequences shown as running totals. The FT's most popular
  interactive that year (over 360,000 visits) and an award winner for visual digital storytelling.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** Second-person framing makes self-identification instant ("you are a founder
  with N people"). Use one decision at most (see A2's caution), with the consequences shown as numbers
  in big type.

## C. Self-identification: one question, and you know if it is for you

### C1. "NSA Files: Decoded" (Gabriel Dance and Ewen MacAskill, The Guardian, 1 Nov 2013)
- **URL:** https://www.theguardian.com/world/interactive/2013/nov/01/snowden-nsa-files-surveillance-revelations-decoded  · `Checked` (by search)
- **First 5 seconds:** The title promises "what the revelations mean for you". Its best-known part, the
  three-degrees tool, asks one thing: how many Facebook friends do you have? Type a number and a much,
  much larger number comes back: everyone within three hops of you.
- **Technique:** A personal input feeding a combinatorial explosion. The surprise is the size of the
  output relative to the small input. World Press Photo digital storytelling prize, 2014.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** Almost the same maths. "How many people are on your team?" (BL L310) and
  back comes the pathways number: 8 → 28, 12 → 66, 15 → 105, 40 → 780. The visitor discovers the
  thesis in their own number.

### C2. "Are you in the American middle class?" (Pew Research Center, 2016–2024 versions)
- **URL:** https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/09/16/are-you-in-the-american-middle-class/  · `Checked`
- **First 5 seconds:** The headline is the question. Three inputs (where you live, household income,
  household size). The answer is a label: which tier you are in, and where you sit among people like you.
- **Technique:** A calculator whose output is a *location*, not a score. Widely shared because everyone
  wants to know their label.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** The Bootloader's Step 0 is this calculator already (BL L318–326): one
  number places you on the destination map, each zone with its own verbatim line (see technique 1).

### C3. "How Y'all, Youse and You Guys Talk" dialect quiz (Josh Katz and Wilson Andrews, NYT, Dec 2013)
- **URL:** https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2014/upshot/dialect-quiz-map.html  · `Known`
- **First 5 seconds:** One plain multiple-choice question about how you say a word. Answer, next
  question. At the end, a heat map of where people talk like you. One of the most-visited pieces the
  NYT has published.
- **Technique:** Questions about *you* before any content; the result is a picture of you.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** Open with the question, not with the book. People cannot resist answering a
  question about themselves, and the answer tells them in one step whether the book is theirs.

### C4. "How Much Hotter Is Your Hometown Than When You Were Born?" (NYT with Climate Impact Lab, Aug 2018)
- **URL:** https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2018/08/30/climate/how-much-hotter-is-your-hometown.html  · `Checked` (by search; exact URL from memory)
- **First 5 seconds:** Two inputs (hometown, birth year), then the article rewrites itself around you: a
  chart of hot days through your lifetime with your birth year marked.
- **Technique:** Personalised reveal: the same story, re-told with your numbers in it.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** After the team-size answer, the rest of the page reads that number: the web
  draws N people, the chart marks your N, the zone line is your zone's.

## D. One physical or data metaphor carries the whole idea

### D1. "Wealth, shown to scale" (Matt Korostoff, 2020)
- **URL:** https://mkorostoff.github.io/1-pixel-wealth/  · `Checked` (by search)
- **First 5 seconds:** "1 pixel = $1,000", a tiny block for ordinary wealth, then an instruction to
  scroll sideways. The scroll goes on and on; a running counter and short notes appear in the band.
- **Technique:** Scroll distance *is* the quantity. Your thumb feels the size of the number.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** Make the visitor scroll through pathways, not paragraphs: a few lines at
  4 people, 66 at 12, 780 at 40. The physical effort of scrolling through the Graveyard is the argument.

### D2. "If the Moon Were Only 1 Pixel" (Josh Worth, 2014)
- **URL:** https://www.joshworth.com/dev/pixelspace/pixelspace_solarsystem.html  · `Known`
- **First 5 seconds:** The sun as an enormous disc, then empty black and a sideways scroll at true
  scale. Short lines of text appear in the void to keep you company.
- **Technique:** Emptiness as information; text placed *in* the space it describes.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** The stretch from 15 to 39 people as a long, dark, mostly empty band with one
  verbatim line at a time inside it: "The Graveyard is not a temporary problem. It's a structural trap."
  (BL L3451).

### D3. The Deep Sea (Neal Agarwal, Dec 2019)
- **URL:** https://neal.fun/deep-sea/  · `Checked` (by search)
- **First 5 seconds:** The ocean surface and a depth counter in metres. Scroll down: it gets darker, the
  zones are named, creatures appear at their real depths.
- **Technique:** A meter pinned to the edge, named zones, a colour ramp that tells you where you are
  before you read anything.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** A team-size meter from 1 to 40+, with the Bootloader's own zones as the
  bands: Solopreneur (1), Small Team (2–3), Valley (4–7), Team (8–12), Threshold (13–14), The Graveyard
  (15–39), Team of Teams (40+) (BL L318–326). Light through 12, darkening after.

### D4. The Fallen of World War II (Neil Halloran, 2015)
- **URL:** http://www.fallen.io/ww2/  · `Known`
- **First 5 seconds:** A narrated data film: small soldier figures stack into columns by country; one
  column grows so tall the camera has to pan up to follow it.
- **Technique:** Counting made physical by stacking identical units; camera movement to show scale.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** Use for *numbers*, not people-blocks: the pathways count stacking as hairlines
  is the scale shock. Caution: this is the tower metaphor the old site used; do not rebuild a tower.

### D5. "Your Life in Weeks" (Tim Urban, Wait But Why, May 2014)
- **URL:** https://waitbutwhy.com/2014/05/life-weeks.html  · `Known`
- **First 5 seconds:** A grid of small boxes, one per week of a 90-year life. Instantly finite,
  countable and personal.
- **Technique:** A grid of identical cells turns an abstract total into something you can see the end of.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** A 12×12 grid with the 66 cells of a 12-person team filled is a striking,
  quiet picture of "66 pathways"; add people and the filled triangle grows by a whole row each time.

### D6. Climate Clock (Gan Golan and Andrew Boyd, Sep 2020)
- **URL:** https://climateclock.world/  · `Checked` (by search)
- **First 5 seconds:** One huge number (the deadline) and a second number (the lifeline). The makers
  wanted "a very spare clock face". Nothing else competes.
- **Technique:** A single number as the whole hero, with one supporting number.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** "12" as the hero numeral and "66" as its companion, with one verbatim line:
  "12 isn't a goal. It's a protective limit." (BL L2210).

## E. Book, manifesto and type sites where the words are the design

### E1. Manifesto for Agile Software Development (2001)
- **URL:** https://agilemanifesto.org/  · `Known`
- **First 5 seconds:** Four lines in the form "X over Y", the left side heavy, the right side light. The
  whole philosophy fits on one screen and has been quoted for 25 years.
- **Technique:** Contrast pairs; weight carries the value judgement.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** The Bootloader has its own contrast table, word for word: "Traditional
  Logic (Linear Growth)" vs "SUSD Logic (Cyclical Growth)" (BL L1424–1440), e.g. "Plateaus are crises
  to push through" / "Plateaus are destinations to optimize"; "Hire to fix growth problems" /
  "Consolidate before adding more". Set the inherited side light and struck, the SUSD side heavy.

### E2. Basecamp's books: *It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work* and *Shape Up*
- **URL:** https://basecamp.com/books (listing) and https://basecamp.com/shapeup/3.4-chapter-13 (hill chart)  · `Checked`
- **First 5 seconds:** The *Calm* entry is a stack of short negated beliefs: "Chaos shouldn't be the
  natural state at work." "Anxiety isn't a prerequisite for progress." "Sitting in meetings all day
  isn't required for success." *Shape Up* is a free web book whose signature figure is the hill chart:
  one hill, uphill "figuring out what our approach is", downhill "the downhill phase of execution",
  and a dot on it.
- **Technique:** (a) The book sells itself with the beliefs it overturns, one line each. (b) A single
  simple shape (a hill) carries a work rhythm so well it became a product feature.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** (a) Stack the book's inversions: "Plateaus are destinations to optimize,
  not crises to push through" / "Scaling is margin expansion, not size expansion" (BL L258–259).
  (b) Up, Side, Down as one simple shape with a dot moving along it, each lap starting higher:
  "The growth is real. The path is seasonal." (BL L851).

### E3. Stripe Press
- **URL:** https://press.stripe.com/  · `Checked` (by search)
- **First 5 seconds:** A dark page of book covers and spines treated as fine objects; classical book
  typography; very little UI.
- **Technique:** Publishing-house restraint: the book's own typography and a manuscript feel, with no
  marketing chrome.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** The *feel* James liked in "Index" ("more like a manuscript, like a book"
  [#8]): a book serif, real typographic detail (small caps, old-style figures, hanging punctuation).
  Caution: at Stripe Press the object is the hero; for SUSD the sentence must be.

### E4. Making Software (Dan Hollick)
- **URL:** https://makingsoftware.com/  · `Checked` (exists; rate-limited tonight; details from a review)
- **First 5 seconds:** A reference-manual book about how software works, sold through a landing page
  of custom-coded animated technical illustrations.
- **Technique:** Hand-built diagram animation (not stock, not AI) as the craft signal for a book.
- **Transferable to SUSD:** The craft bar for any diagram (the web, the curve, the cycle): engineered,

### E5. Mona Sans and Hubot Sans (GitHub, Nov 2022)
- **URL:** https://github.com/mona-sans  · `Checked`
- **First 5 seconds:** "Two files." "Thousands of variations." "Infinite possibilities." in very large
  type, with the weight, width and slant axes demonstrated by the letters themselves.
- **Technique:** Variable-font axes as motion: the type moves, so the words are the animated object.

- **Transferable to SUSD:** UP, SIDE and DOWN performed by the type: UP rises and gains weight, SIDE
  widens on the width axis, DOWN condenses and settles. The three words looping are the cycle; no

## 15 transferable techniques (Vite + React, canvas / SVG / WebGL / CSS; no external images, no AI imagery)

General notes for all of them:
- Pin exact versions (e.g. `react@18.3.1`, `react-dom@18.3.1`). Prefer no animation library; a 40-line
  spring or `requestAnimationFrame` lerp is enough for everything below.
- Every animated piece needs a `prefers-reduced-motion` path that shows the end state.
- Canvas: scale by `Math.min(devicePixelRatio, 2)`; pause loops with `IntersectionObserver` when
  offscreen; seed any randomness (mulberry32) so screenshots are repeatable.
- The H1 is always real text in the DOM (the sentence), even when a canvas sits behind it.
- Copy rule: any sentence is verbatim from `src_copy.ts` or `public_bootloader.md` with its line number
  in a comment; computed values are numerals and labels only.

### 1. The Locating Question (one input, you are placed on the map)
From C1, C2, C3. The page opens on "How many people are on your team?" (BL L310), set huge, with a big
numeric stepper/slider (1 to 40+). The answer returns a zone label and that zone's verbatim line from
the Bootloader: Solopreneur "Founder only. No coordination overhead." / Small Team "Leader + helpers.
Coordination manageable." / Valley "Transition zone between Small Team and Team." / Team "Leader +
team. Still know everyone's name." (BL L1246–1249) / Threshold "WARNING: You are at the Graveyard
threshold. Growing to 15-16 enters structurally dangerous territory." (BL L3449) / Graveyard "The
Graveyard is not a temporary problem. It's a structural trap." (BL L3451) / 40+ "This bootloader is
built for Rented-Scale businesses with 1-12 employees." (BL L4252).
*Build:* `useState(n)`; zone lookup table typed from BL L318–326; `<input type="range">` restyled plus
+/− buttons and arrow keys; huge `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` numeral; `aria-live="polite"` on
the result. Default n = 8 so the first screen is already meaningful before anyone touches it.

### 2. Live numerals beside verbatim sentences (reactive document)
From A3, C4. The visitor's number flows into every figure on the page: pathways = n(n-1)/2, the zone,
the marked point on the chart. The sentence ("66 pathways" is the canonical label, BL L2216) never
changes; the numeral next to it counts.
*Build:* one React context for `n`; derived values memoised; numerals tween with a spring
(stiffness ~170, damping ~26) to avoid jumps; tabular figures so widths do not jitter.

### 3. The pathways web (complete graph), drawn live
From B1, A1, C1. N points on a ring, every pair joined: 28 lines at 8, 66 at 12, 105 at 15, 780 at 40.
At 12 it is still a delicate web; past 15 it turns into a dark solid. This *is* "complexity grows faster
than capacity" with no caption needed.
*Build:* Canvas 2D; nodes on a circle; per-line alpha = `clamp(k / pathways, 0.04, 0.6)` so density
reads as darkness; colour shifts from ink to the redline red past 12; a new node fades in and its
n−1 lines draw out from it over ~400 ms. 780 lines is trivial for canvas. Keep it lower contrast than
the H1 so the words stay brightest.

### 4. Draw your guess, then reveal
From B3. A blank chart: x = people (1 to 40), y = coordination. "Draw it." The visitor drags a line
(most draw a straight one); release reveals the true curve, the straight capacity line and the crossing,
labelled with "Complexity > Capacity = Redline." (M L2997).
*Build:* SVG; `pointerdown/move/up` with `setPointerCapture`; snap x to integers, keep a y array;
reveal the real path with `stroke-dasharray` / `stroke-dashoffset`; "Skip" shows the answer; on touch
devices, a larger hit area and `touch-action: none` on the chart only.

### 5. Old map / new map contrast stack
From E1, E2. Rows from the Bootloader contrast table (BL L1424–1440) and inversions (BL L258–265):
inherited side light and struck through, SUSD side heavy. One row per beat, or the whole stack as one
poster-like screen.
*Build:* CSS grid two columns (stacked on phone with the struck line above); the strike is an SVG line
animated with `stroke-dashoffset`; the SUSD side animates `font-weight` 300 to 800 on a variable font.

### 6. Variable-axis type that performs Up, Side, Down
From E5. The words are the animation: UP grows taller and heavier, SIDE widens, DOWN condenses and
sinks, then repeat ("Up. Side. Down. Repeat." M L1713).
*Build:* a variable font with `wght` and `wdth` (Mona Sans, OFL; or Roboto Flex / Anybody from Google
Fonts); CSS `@keyframes` on `font-variation-settings` plus a small `translateY` / `scaleY`. Animating
variation settings relayouts each frame, so keep it to three words, give them `contain: layout paint`,
and fall back to transforms only on low-end devices.

### 7. The climbing cycle (each lap ends higher)
From A5, E2. A path that goes up, along, down, and ends higher than it started, repeated three times
like a staircase of hills; a dot travels it with the labels Up / Side / Down riding the path. Line:
"Each cycle deposits systems and margin. The next cycle withdraws from a larger account." (BL L866).
*Build:* SVG path; dot positioned with `getPointAtLength` on a time loop (or CSS `offset-path`);
labels on `<textPath>`; reduced motion shows the full path with arrowheads.

### 8. Scale scroll with named zones and a meter
From D1, D2, D3. A tall section where scroll position is team size: a pinned counter (people, pathways)
and full-bleed colour bands for the zones; light to 12, then the long dark Graveyard band with one
verbatim line at a time.
*Build:* sticky container (`position: sticky; top: 0`) inside a ~600vh section; scroll progress from
`getBoundingClientRect` in a passive `scroll` listener with rAF; map progress to n with an easing that
gives 1–12 most of the distance; snap points at zone edges (`scroll-snap-type: y proximity`). Optional:
CSS scroll-driven animations where supported, JS fallback elsewhere.

### 9. One giant number as the hero
From D6. "12" at 40–60vh (or "66" beside it), everything else orbiting it. Line: "12 isn't a goal. It's
a protective limit." (BL L2210).
*Build:* `font-size: clamp(10rem, 38vw, 34rem)`; tabular, lining figures; an optical-size axis if the
font has one; a slow light sweep across the numeral with a masked `linear-gradient` (CSS only).

### 10. A small simulation that generates its own chart
From B1. People drift in a field; each new hire links to everyone already there; a two-line chart (people
vs pathways) draws itself above from the simulation's own state. Re-run at 8, 12, 15, 40 with one control.
*Build:* Canvas 2D; simple Euler integration with soft walls; seeded PRNG; spatial hashing is not
needed under ~60 agents; chart in SVG bound to the same state; pause offscreen and under reduced motion.

### 11. One decision with consequences (no game chrome)
From A2, B5. At 12 people: three buttons, Up (hire), Side (systemise), Down (cut). Up adds a node and
its 12 new lines; Side bundles lines into system hubs; Down removes load. The visitor discovers the
cycle by choosing. Supporting line: "You can't build a building with only height. You need all three
dimensions or it topples." (BL L816).
*Build:* `useReducer` state machine (people, systems, load); all visuals derived from state; no scores,
no levels, no avatars (James: "it can't look like a game").

### 12. The Bootloader as a physical file, not a prompt
From B4. A rendered "file" with filename, version (V16) and live word count (35,445) imported at build,
stacked sheets with real depth, and one dramatic opt-in action that turns its stamp to "Yours". Lines:
"The pages are reference. The file is the operating system." (M L4497).
*Build:* `import md from '../public/bootloader.md?raw'` and count words at build time (Vite);
CSS 3D (`perspective`, layered `transform: translateZ`) for the sheets, no images; the button is the
file itself (press, sheet lifts, stamp lands) with a 150–250 ms spring.

### 13. A shareable personal result
From C1, C2, C3. The result is encoded in the URL (`?team=9`), so "I'm at 9, in the Team destination"
is a link that opens the page set to 9. Seven static share cards, one per zone, made from type only.
*Build:* `URLSearchParams` + `history.replaceState`; per-zone OG images rendered at build from SVG to
PNG with a Node script (e.g. `@resvg/resvg-js`), set in the HTML head per route or via a small edge
function on Vercel. No faces, no AI images.

### 14. One sentence per screen, centred, with the load-bearing word marked
From A4, E1, E2. Each screen holds a single verbatim sentence, very large and centred, with the word that
carries the idea in a contrasting weight or colour ("Redline" in red, "Oxygen" in the Up colour). This is
the direct answer to "you just look at the blocks and don't read the text".
*Build:* `text-wrap: balance`; `clamp()` sizes so a sentence never exceeds ~4 lines on phone; words split
into spans and revealed with a 30–40 ms stagger on `IntersectionObserver`; optional `scroll-snap`.

### 15. Depth carried by the type itself
From TASTE (James wants "real rendering and depth") and E5. The words get dimension and light instead of
sitting beside a render: an extruded or letterpress headline with a raking light that follows the pointer.
*Build (light):* CSS stacked `text-shadow` extrusion (8–12 layers) plus a pointer-driven gradient
highlight via `background-clip: text`. *Build (heavy):* `three@0.160.0` with SDF text (troika-three-text),
one directional light, and the camera fixed so the type stays readable; DOM H1 underneath for SEO and
screen readers; static fallback on low-power devices.

## Anti-references (what to avoid, and why)

- **Apple-style product pages** (banned by James [#4]): a hero object with a small sentence. Same failure
  as the blocks.
- **Snow Fall-style photo scrollytelling** (NYT, 2012): pictures lead and the text rides on them. Wrong
  hierarchy for this brief, and no photos or AI imagery are allowed here.
- **WebGL showcase sites** whose spectacle is the point. James's most repeated verdict: "cool, but ...
  nothing to do with his book" [#8].
- **Game UI** (scores, avatars, levels) even when the mechanic is right (A2, B5).
- **Stripe Press's rotating book object as hero** (E3) and **the Fallen's stacking tower** (D4): borrow the
  restraint and the counting, not the object.
