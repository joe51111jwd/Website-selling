"Why now" is the slide most often answered with an adjective. The industry is *changing*, the market is *ready*, the technology is *transforming* things. All of that might be true, and none of it explains why this company, built this year, has an opening that didn't exist before.

Investors ask the question because timing is one of the few things that can make a small team beat larger, better-funded ones. If the opening is real, the founder should be able to name it.

## Three kinds of good answer

**A rule changed.** A regulation, a standard or a requirement now forces behaviour that used to be optional. These come with a date, which makes them easy to believe.

**A cost fell.** Something that used to be too expensive to do at small scale is now cheap. That opens up customers who couldn't be served before.

**A behaviour shifted.** Customers now expect or accept something they didn't. The buying habit exists, and the incumbents haven't caught up.

The strongest answers combine two of these. The weakest answers name a trend that's equally true for every company in the sector, so it explains nothing about yours.

## Make it specific enough to check

A good "why now" slide has dates, numbers or named events on it. An investor should be able to look any of them up after the meeting and find them true. That's what turns the slide from mood into evidence.

## In the example

The original slide had three trends any healthcare company could claim. The rewrite has one rule with a date, one cost change, and one shift in patient behaviour. Together they explain why a ten-practitioner clinic needs this software in 2027 and didn't in 2022.
