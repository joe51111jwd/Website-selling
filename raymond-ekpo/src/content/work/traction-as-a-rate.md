Totals are comfortable to put on a slide because they only go up. That's exactly why investors discount them. A total hides time (was that two months or two years?), quality (do they stay?) and source (did you buy them?).

At pre-seed and seed, nobody expects big numbers. What investors look for is evidence that something is starting to work and a sense of how fast. A rate, a cohort and a source tell them that. A total doesn't.

## Three things to show instead

**Rate.** Growth over a stated period, from a stated base. "Up from 13 to 41 in six months" says more than "41" ever could.

**Quality.** Whether the people who arrive stay and keep using the product. Retention by cohort is the clearest version. If it's too early for cohorts, show depth of use: how often, how much, by whom.

**Source.** Where the growth comes from. Referrals and word of mouth suggest the product is doing the selling. Paid acquisition is fine, but investors will want the cost next to it.

## If you're pre-revenue

Traction doesn't have to be revenue. Design partners who have committed time, letters of intent with real terms, a waitlist that converts, pilots with usage data. The same rule applies to all of them: show the rate and the quality, not just the count.

## In the example

"2,000+ users" turns out to include trial accounts that never booked a patient. The rewrite leads with the 41 clinics that pay, which is a smaller number with a much better story. It's growing fast, customers stay, and most of the growth comes from customers bringing in other customers.
